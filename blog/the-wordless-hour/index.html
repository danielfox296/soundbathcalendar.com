The wordless hour

My ex-wife used to introduce me as someone who was great at making money. We have been divorced for years. I still say it to myself most afternoons.

By Daniel · July 25, 2026

My ex-wife used to introduce me at parties as someone who was great at making money.

She meant it warmly. That is worth a second, because the lines people never manage to shake are usually the kind ones. It arrived at a dinner table, in front of others, in the tone you use for something obviously true and mildly impressive. I stood there and let it happen. There is no graceful way to object to a compliment, so nobody does, which is part of how this works.

What a compliment asks of you

Set it beside the most common introduction there is. This is my brother, he's a good guy. That one describes a state, and you can be a good guy while asleep. Great at making money describes money arriving, and money has to keep arriving. There is no past tense available for it. A strong year does not retire the claim. A strong year resets the clock and starts the next exam.

The second thing took me longer to see. Money is weather. Clients leave for reasons that have nothing to do with you, an industry turns, a good business dies in a bad year because the year was bad. When someone says you are great at making money, they have put the money inside you, like height. So when the money does not come, I have nowhere to put it except on my own character.

What I lost was the slow season. A farmer gets a fallow year and nobody thinks the farmer has failed. A man who is great at making money does not get one. He fails for twelve months, in front of an audience, and files it under personal defect.

She left. I kept saying it.

We have been divorced a long time. She has not said that line about me in years. She is not in the room, not in my phone, not in my life in any ordinary sense.

I still say it. Most afternoons, in my own voice, usually while looking at a bank balance.

That is the part worth noticing. One person said it a handful of times, more than a decade ago. Every repetition since has been mine. You make a borrowed sentence permanent yourself, in your own head, and your own voice carries an advantage nobody else's does. When your mother tells you who you are, some part of you pushes back. When you tell yourself at three in the afternoon, in your own timbre, it does not register as a claim at all. It sounds like noticing.

I also say it in the shortest form available. The long version would run something like: I built a couple of things that worked and a couple that did not, in markets I never controlled, over about fifteen years. What I actually say is five words. Great at making money. Several times a day, with almost no surface on it to argue with, which is probably most of why it holds.

The world is made of language

Terence McKenna said that constantly. His wider claim was that you are running a culture you never chose and cannot easily see, the way a fish is in no position to review water. Plenty of his work is a stretch. That part is not, and Wittgenstein got there first and more plainly: a picture held us captive, and we could not get outside it, for it lay in our language.

You can hear the shape of it in any ordinary introduction. He's a good guy. That brother is now unavailable for fury, for walking out, for wanting something ugly, for saying the unforgivable thing at Thanksgiving. Nobody ever told him he could not. He simply never considers them. He works from a shorter list of things a person might do, and calls the shortness his personality.

Nearly every word I use about myself came from somewhere else. A father's assessment. A manager's phrasing at a review. A diagnosis. A partner's word for what I do when I am tired. I did not select any of them. I heard them, found them roughly accurate, started using them, and after enough years I lost track of which ones were mine.

Where Dispenza is useful, and where he stops

Joe Dispenza's version is more mechanical. Repeated thought plus repeated feeling becomes a rehearsed state, and after enough rehearsal your body produces the feeling without waiting for the thought to show up first. Same days, same choices, a future predicted out of a memorized past. As a description of how a sentence gets down into the muscle, it matches what most people can observe in themselves by Wednesday.

He stops being useful at the instruction. Interrupt the thought. Choose a different one.

You would be arguing in the voice you are arguing with. Same courtroom, same man at both tables, and he is also the judge. People work at this for years. What many of them end up holding is a second sentence, about how they are bad at changing the first one.

Why sound rather than silence

Silent meditation leaves the narrator alone in an empty room with excellent acoustics. Mine gets more articulate in there.

Inner speech is speech. It runs on much of the same equipment you use for talking out loud, down to small movements in the throat. Hearing yourself think is closer to literal than most people assume, which means something else can occupy the channel.

Put words in the music and he sings along. Put a melody in it and he predicts the next bar. A gong fills the room completely and gives him nothing to say about it: no lyric to follow, no chorus arriving on schedule, and you keep almost resolving the overtones and then losing them again. Nobody hums along to a gong. An hour is also longer than he has material for. Somewhere in the middle he runs out and stops for a while.

Hold the mechanics of that loosely. Inner speech and subvocal articulation are well established, covered further in what the science actually says. Whether a gong occupies that same channel is a reasonable guess rather than a settled finding, and this corner of the world already has enough confident claims in it. What I can report is what happens in the room.

An hour where nothing gets counted

Here is the specific mercy for a man carrying my sentence. In that room, lying still is the assigned activity. Producing nothing is what everyone came to do. There is no number anywhere, nobody asking what I am working on, nothing a person could enter into evidence.

Most weeks, when I rest, I am also gathering evidence against myself. In there I am a guy on the floor.

Worth saying about the hour itself: some facilitators talk through a lot of it. A guided visualization, a chakra named for each bowl, an intention spoken out loud at the start. That is a preference, and plenty of people want it. For the thing described here, the less anyone says, the more room there is. If a listing reads heavy on talk and you are going for the reason in this essay, ask the facilitator what the ratio is before you book.

What happens afterward

The first twenty minutes are usually pure narration. Arrive expecting a blank mind and you will spend the hour grading yourself on how blank it is, which is the same exam wearing different clothes.

What you get is a contrast. You do not hear the refrigerator until it cuts out. Most people have never once had their internal dialogue stop, so they have never had a chance to recognize it as a sound. When you start up again, and you start up fast, you hear it.

That is the whole of it. I say the sentence again, and I catch myself in the act of saying it instead of receiving it as a report on the world. It gets filed under things I say a lot. It is still there. I am just not standing on it.

At the last session I went to, I said it in the parking lot. In her voice, at a dinner table, sometime around 2016. I heard myself do it, which was new.

Then I got in the car and checked my balance before I had closed the door.
