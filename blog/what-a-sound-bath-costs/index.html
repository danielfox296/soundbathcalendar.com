What a sound bath costs

Across the 130 sessions on the calendar right now, the median ticket is $40 and the middle of the range runs $25 to $65. A third of listings never say what they cost at all.

Report · By Sound Bath Calendar · August 24, 2026

<!-- Every figure below was computed from the ItemList the calendar publishes on its own root page: 130 upcoming sessions running 2026-08-24 through 2026-09-13, pulled 2026-08-24. Method matches the monthly roundup: the numbers are frozen at the pull date and stated as such, not regenerated at build time. Refresh them if this gets edited later. -->

The median sound bath on the Front Range costs $40. Most of them fall between $25 and $65. Eleven of the sessions on the calendar right now are free.

That is the whole answer. The rest of this is where those numbers come from and what moves a listing to one end or the other.

How many listings actually say

There are 130 sessions on the calendar as this goes up, running from today through September 13. Seventy-six of them publish a price. Fifty-four do not.

That last number matters more than the median does. Two out of five listings ask you to write to someone, follow a ticket link, or show up and find out. If you are trying to plan a week around a budget, the gap in the data is the thing you are actually up against, and no median fixes it.

Of the 76 that do say, 11 are free. The remaining 65 have a number attached, and those 65 are what the median describes.

The range

The cheapest ticketed session on the calendar is $15. The most expensive is $71.

Between those, the distribution is tighter than the endpoints suggest. Eight out of ten priced sessions sit between $25 and $65, and the single most common prices are $33, $40, $45, $60 and $65. There is no cluster at the bottom and no cluster at the top. Front Range sound baths are priced like yoga classes, not like concerts.

What moves the number, and what does not

Two things separate a $25 session from a $65 one. A third thing that everyone assumes matters does not.

Length does. Seven listings state a run time longer than an hour, 75 or 90 minutes or two hours. Their median is $65, against $40 for the calendar as a whole. Seven is a small number and the signal is only as good as the listings that bother to say, but it points one direction and nothing points back.

Venue does, at the free end. Five of the 11 free sessions are at a public library, a park or a community space. Only 6 of the 65 paid sessions are. Free sound baths on the Front Range are mostly a library-and-parks phenomenon, which also means they thin out when the weather turns.

Add-ons do not. This is the one worth knowing before you book. Thirty-two of the 65 priced listings pair the sound with something else, reiki, breathwork, restorative postures, cacao or tea. Their median is $40. The 33 listings offering sound and nothing else have a median of $44.52. Adding a second modality does not add to the ticket, and on this calendar it correlates with a slightly cheaper one.

If cost is the barrier

Eleven free sessions are on the calendar right now, and more listings are donation-based or sliding scale without publishing a figure. Both are filterable on the calendar itself, and the map shows where each one is.

Free sessions concentrate in parks, libraries and community spaces, which means they cluster in summer and thin out as the weather turns. If cost is the constraint, the next few weeks are the better few weeks.

How this was counted

One source: the ItemList the calendar publishes on its own root page, pulled August 24, 2026, covering 130 sessions through September 13. A session counts as priced only if it publishes a number. Free means a listed price of zero. Donation and sliding-scale listings that publish no figure are counted as not publishing a price, which is why the free count here runs lower than the number of sessions you can actually attend without paying.

Listings change constantly. The calendar is always the current version of the truth.
